## Motivation

WP19 v0.24 passed the intervalization-design gate for the goal-oriented adjoint route. Before intervalizing a terminal objective containing absolute values and a normalizing ratio, v0.25 returns to the polynomial reduction already proved in v0.9–v0.11.

The frozen C500 architecture has support entirely inside P11. Once the K36 endpoint sign chamber is certified, the fixed signed quantity

`H_C500 = sum_K36 sigma_g n_g - 9 sum_C500 tau_g n_g`

is a polynomial numerator whose negativity implies negativity of the C500 upper surrogate whenever the separately tracked normalizer is nonzero.

v0.10 rigorously certified this signed numerator at N11–N13. The purpose of v0.25 is to compose the already certified N14–N18 Galerkin trajectory radii with the same exact-rational endpoint perturbation calculation, with no retuning.

## Endpoint certification

For each `N=14,...,18`, v0.25 downloads the completed same-datum Arb certificate, extracts its certified terminal L2 trajectory-error radius, and feeds that radius into the existing exact-rational v0.10 endpoint perturbation code.

A cutoff passes only if:

- all 36 K36 numerator signs are locked throughout the certified endpoint ball;
- the signed C500 numerator upper bound is strictly negative;
- the normalizer perturbation lower bound remains strictly positive;
- the frozen witness, K36 and C500 hashes match exactly.

## Why this matters for the next adjoint

If v0.25 passes through N18, the next interval-adjoint stage can target the fixed polynomial signed numerator rather than the non-smooth `F11` ratio objective. The normalizer remains a separate already-certified guard. This removes two avoidable sources of interval overestimation: absolute-value kinks and division by the normalizer.

## Claim boundary

This is a finite endpoint-composition theorem conditional on the already completed finite-Galerkin trajectory certificates. It does not prove an all-N transfer theorem or a continuum Navier–Stokes result.
